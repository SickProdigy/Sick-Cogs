# Pokemon

Persistent cross-guild Pokémon catching and wild battles for Red.

Player commands use `[p]pokemon`, with `[p]poke` and `[p]pkmn` available as shorter aliases. Setup commands use `[p]pokemonset`, with `[p]pokeset` and `[p]pkmnset` as aliases.

## Included

- One global collection per Discord user across every guild served by the same bot, shown as an alphabetical nine-Pokémon grid with private add/replace party controls.
- Exact Pokémon-for-Pokémon trading with recipient confirmation, 15-minute expiry, immutable instance validation, restart-safe settlement recovery, and automatic party repair.
- Level-1 Bulbasaur, Charmander, or Squirtle starter with a generated laboratory carousel and personalized received-Pokémon reveal; immutable owned-Pokémon IDs; six-member parties; ten 30-slot boxes; Pokédex tracking; and profiles.
- Opt-in spawn channels with persisted jittered timers, bounded channel-aware concurrency, randomized activity thresholds, cooldowns, repeat suppression, owner-bounded generation filters, approachable rarity tiers, 15-minute wild encounter expiry, and administrator recovery.
- All 151 Generation 1 species bundled from validated PokéAPI records, including base experience and growth rates, plus owner-triggered cache sync for later generations.
- A persisted 1-in-4,096 spawn-time shiny roll uses shiny encounter, battle, result, collection, party, profile, progression, and registration sprites without rerolling on capture.
- A generated gold six-slot horizontal party lineup with no backend ownership IDs exposed.
- Generation-aware battles persist a `standard` mechanics ruleset separately from Kanto campaign content, allowing later regions without rewriting owned Pokémon or active battles.
- Persistent level-scaled wild battles with classic Fight/Pokémon/Bag/Run menus, all 147 Red/Blue/Yellow level-up moves used by the original 151 species, authentic four-move progression, PP, accuracy, physical/special damage, priority/level-scaled Speed ordering, ruleset-versioned type effectiveness, same-type attack bonuses, modern critical-hit stages and stat-stage handling, level/IV-scaled combat stats for owned and wild Pokémon, critical hits, classic effectiveness/immunity/status/progression narration, persistent standard-ruleset sleep and confusion durations, type-based status immunities, burn Attack reduction, deterministic thaw/confusion behavior, supported status/stat/healing/fixed-damage/multi-hit/drain/recoil effects, switching, fainting, deterministic action history/recovery, XP, generated personalized outcome and informative first-catch Pokédex registration cards plus evolution and move-learning reveal cards, restart-safe four-move replacement choices, species base-XP rewards, authentic growth curves, conscious-participant XP sharing, and every Gen 1 level-based evolution.
- Generation-aware HP, species catch-rate, status, and ball-modifier calculations with Poké, Great, and Ultra Ball inventory and restart-safe, idempotent settlement.
- An explicit support audit classifies all 147 bundled moves as accurate, deliberately standardized, or deferred; order-sensitive flinching and Haze stat resets are supported.
- A validated catalog-version manifest independently versions typing, stats, learnsets, evolution methods, abilities, items, weather, and terrain; battles and owned Pokémon preserve their content provenance.
- Centered encounter art with in-image name, sex, level, and HP plus generated Game Boy-inspired battle scenes, trainer-avatar victory and catch compositions, bottom-box catch messages, battle-relative defeat and escape scenes, bounded sprite/render caches, and accessible embed text.
- An interactive Pokédex with paginated lists, direct entry selection, search, filters, generation selection, progressive seen/caught detail unlocks, and selectable Retro or Compact presentation.
- Ordered Kanto Gym Leader challenges, persistent badges, and a trainer-profile badge case without introducing a separate trainer XP level.
- Persistent party HP/status, server-configured Pokémon Center channels, and atomic Potion/Revive recovery.
- Red user-data deletion releases affected encounters.

Collections are global only within one bot installation. Separate bots do not share data.

## Setup

1. Load the cog.
2. Grant Embed Links and Attach Files in spawn channels.
3. Enable a spawn channel with `[p]pokemonset channel #channel` and designate any existing healing channel with `[p]pokemonset center #chat`.
4. Review settings with `[p]pokemonset settings` (or `status`). Timed spawning defaults to roughly one encounter per server every 60 minutes, with ±20% jitter and a randomly selected free configured channel. Administrators may make it slower and choose concurrency within the owner ceiling with `[p]pokemonset concurrency <limit>`; the bot owner may use `[p]pokemonownerset timer <1-10080>` for faster per-server timers.
5. Members choose `[p]pokemon starter bulbasaur|charmander|squirtle`.
6. Timed encounters bring members back without requiring existing conversation. The earlier activity system remains available with `[p]pokemonset mode activity`; restore timers with `[p]pokemonset mode timed`.
7. Review the next Kanto Gym with `[p]pokemon gym` and challenge it with `[p]pokemon gym challenge`.

Returning trainers who run bare `[p]pokemon` receive an owner-restricted illustrated main menu featuring their Discord avatar with functional Party, Collection, Pokédex, Bag, Research, Profile, Goals, Gyms, Mart, and Trade controls. The default Retro presentation and a rotating Modern Kanto box-cover presentation share the same controls; switch in place or save a preference with `[p]pokemon menustyle retro|modern`. New trainers continue directly into starter selection. Choosing a Pokémon already in the current party uses “Move to” controls and swaps positions, while boxed Pokémon use “Replace” controls. The placement prompt and buttons show each Pokémon’s level; after a successful change, the collection browser and temporary placement prompt are removed to avoid channel clutter. Collection cards, accessible lists, and selectors mark active party order as `P1` through `P6`. Modern covers include Red, Blue, Yellow, Gold, Silver, Crystal, FireRed, LeafGreen, HeartGold, SoulSilver, and the paired Let’s Go Pikachu/Eevee artwork; they are validated, cached remote presentation assets and are not bundled in this repository.

Player commands include `[p]pokemon collection`, `pokedex`, `pokedexstyle`, `gym`, `gym challenge`, `party`, `party add`, `party remove`, `moves`, `profile [@member]`, `profilestyle retro|gold`, `bag`, `menustyle retro|modern`, `achievements`, `research`, `mart`, `buy <item> [quantity]`, `use potion <slot-or-name>`, `use revive <slot-or-name>`, `center`, `trade @trainer <your collection number> <their collection number>`, and `trade give @trainer <number> [number] [number]`. Defeating a wild Pokémon awards its species/level reward across conscious Pokémon that participated; catching awards half that ruleset-controlled reward and adds the encountered Pokémon at its wild level. Battle Bag shows live quantities for Poké, Great, and Ultra Balls plus Potions and Revives. Medicine targets an eligible party member and consumes the turn, while each ball retains its progressively stronger catch modifier. When a Pokémon already knows four moves, its next species-authentic level-up move pauses for the trainer to choose a move to forget or give up learning it; use `[p]pokemon moves` to reopen an interrupted choice. Individual Pokédex entries track seen, completed battles, defeated, caught, and escaped totals. The first successful catch of each species displays a dedicated Pokédex registration card; repeat catches skip it. SickGaming VIP membership can be linked by the bot owner to one trusted Discord guild and role. Eligible members receive an idempotent monthly pack on their first Pokémon activity each UTC month (20 Poké Balls, 5 Great Balls, 2 Ultra Balls, 10 Potions, and 3 Revives by default) and receive 480 PC-storage slots instead of 240, in addition to the six-Pokémon party. Losing VIP never deletes Pokémon; an over-limit trainer simply cannot store another catch until the owned total falls below the normal 246-Pokémon limit, while the catch still advances Pokédex statistics and consumes the used ball. One-time collection goals at 5, 10, 25, 50, and 100 unique species award progressively stronger Poké Balls; victory goals at the same milestones award Potions and Revives. Completed-encounter goals at 10, 25, 50, 100, 250, and 500 add modest ball supplies, while type-specialist goals at 3, 5, and 10 unique species sharing a type award two Poké Balls, one Great Ball, and one Ultra Ball respectively. Dual-type species contribute once to each of their types, and repeat catches do not advance type-specialist goals. Use `[p]pokemon achievements` to view permanent milestone progress. `[p]pokemon research` shows three daily Professor tasks that reset at 00:00 UTC: complete three encounters for two Poké Balls, win two battles for two Potions, and catch one Pokémon for one Great Ball. Daily and permanent rewards are granted automatically when battles settle. The Poké Mart also sells Poké Balls, Great Balls, Ultra Balls, Potions, and Revives through Red's configured global or server-local bank. Default prices are 50, 150, 300, 75, and 400 credits respectively, calibrated around Economy's default 120-credit payday; the bot owner can tune each with `[p]pokemonownerset martprice <item> <price>`. The bundled Generation 1 roster works immediately. The bot owner can populate a later-generation cache with `[p]pokemonownerset catalogsync <1-9>`; sync selects level-up learnsets from that generation’s game versions and labels the resulting cache with its catalog release.


## Encounter fairness and control

Timed spawning is the default: each enabled server schedules an encounter around every hour with ±20% jitter, posted to one randomly selected configured channel that does not already contain an encounter or Gym battle. Server administrators may choose a base interval from the bot-owner-configured minimum through one week; fresh installations use a 15-minute administrator floor while each server still starts at a 60-minute selected interval; the bot owner sets that floor with `[p]pokemonownerset timermin` and may explicitly override an individual server from 1 minute through one week. Servers default to one active encounter. Server administrators may choose concurrency within the bot-wide ceiling; the fresh-install ceiling is five; the bot owner sets that ceiling from one through five with `[p]pokemonownerset globalconcurrency <1-5>` and may override an individual server up to five with `[p]pokemonownerset concurrency <1-5>`. When every slot or configured channel is occupied, the due spawn waits for capacity rather than being discarded. Schedules and active-slot usage persist across restarts and are visible through `[p]pokemonset settings`. The optional activity mode counts qualifying conversation and successfully completed member commands in configured spawn channels; bot output, failed commands, and rapid repeats are ignored. It retains bounded thresholds and cooldowns for servers that prefer it. Ordinary wild levels follow the strongest Pokémon owned by trainers active in that server during the previous five minutes, using a weighted range from two levels below through four above and a level-100 ceiling; without recent trainers they begin near level 1. Level-evolved species enter the ordinary pool only when the selected wild level reaches their evolution requirement, so stronger active trainers gradually unlock higher-stage encounters. Species rarity remains independent of level. Wild encounters remain open for 15 minutes by default; unattended cards are deleted by default. Server administrators may use `[p]pokemonset expiredcards keep` to retain the existing named, control-free, visibly dimmed got-away card, or `delete` to restore automatic removal. The bot owner controls that lifetime, the friendly/standard/challenging rarity curve, available generations, activity-mode floors, and whether legendary or mythical species may enter ordinary spawns. Special species are event-only by default. Manual server spawns obey the selected mode’s rate limit; the bot owner retains a testing bypass and may use `[p]pokemonset spawn shiny` to force only the shiny state while exercising the normal encounter flow.

Server controls remain under `[p]pokemonset` (aliases `pokeset` and `pkmnset`), including `encountertime <minutes|default>` for choosing or inheriting the wild-card lifetime within the bot-wide range. Bot-wide controls are separated under `[p]pokemonownerset` (aliases `pokeownerset` and `pkmnownerset`), including `viprole <guild-id> <role-id>`, `vipclear`, `vippack <balls> <great> <ultra> <potions> <revives>`, `timermin <1-10080>`, `encounterlimits <1-15> <1-15>`, `globalconcurrency <1-5>`, `globalstatus` (aliases `settings` and `status`), `encountertime <minutes>`, `globallimits <activity> <seconds>`, `globalgenerations <1-9...>`, `rarity friendly|standard|challenging`, and `specials true|false`. `globalstatus` reports only bot-wide values and command boundaries—including the configured administrator timer floor and owner override range, Center cooldown, activity floors, generations, rarity, specials, default Pokédex style, and Mart prices—while effective guild settings remain under `[p]pokemonset status`. Server battle duration remains adjustable through `[p]pokemonset battleexpiry <minutes>`. The bot owner sets the inherited lifetime with `[p]pokemonownerset encountertime <minutes>` and bounds server choices with `encounterlimits`; fresh installs allow 1–15 minutes and default to 15. Bot owners can reset a development-test profile with `[p]pokemonownerset resetplayer @user confirm`; this clears that user's complete Pokémon progress only.

## Healing and persistent health

Battle HP and primary status—including remaining sleep turns—persist on owned Pokémon; confusion is volatile and clears on switching. Use `[p]pokemon center` from the server's configured Pokémon Center channel for free full-party HP, status, PP, and fainting recovery. The command shows a five-second restoration-room scene and then offers the trainer a **Collect Party** button. Healing is saved before the animation begins, so a restart or deleted message cannot strand the party. Center use has a bot-wide 30-minute per-user cooldown by default; the bot owner can set 1–1440 minutes with `[p]pokemonownerset centercooldown <minutes>`. Outside a Center, `[p]pokemon use potion <slot-or-name>` restores up to 20 HP to a conscious Pokémon and `[p]pokemon use revive <slot-or-name>` returns a fainted Pokémon at half HP. New trainers receive ten Poké Balls, three Great Balls, one Ultra Ball, five Potions, and two Revives; the versioned migration adds the new ball tiers without replacing existing inventory. Item consumption and health updates are stored together. The unrestricted development `heal` command has been removed.

## Trading

Use `[p]pokemon trade collection @trainer [page]` to inspect another trainer’s alphabetized, numbered choices before proposing an exchange.

Use `[p]pokemon trade give @trainer <number> [number] [number]` to give one to three exact Pokémon without receiving one back. Gifts require recipient confirmation, available storage at acceptance, and leave the giver with at least one Pokémon. The three-Pokémon maximum keeps bulk transfers deliberate.

Use `[p]pokemon trade @trainer <your collection number> <their collection number>` to offer one exact Pokémon for one exact Pokémon from another trainer’s alphabetized collection. The offer shows both Pokémon’s name, shiny state, sex, and level. Both exchanges and gifts require the recipient to accept; either trainer may decline or cancel, and unanswered offers expire after 15 minutes. Pokémon reserved by one pending trade cannot be reused in another; a Discord delivery failure immediately releases both reservations. Both trainers must finish active battles before offering or accepting. Settlement is journaled before either collection changes, serialized in a stable user-lock order, replay-safe after partial storage failure, and recovered when the cog loads. The received Pokémon goes to storage unless the outgoing Pokémon was the trainer’s only party member, in which case it takes that party slot. Pending offers and unresolved settlement journals are terminally cancelled if either trainer’s data is deleted, preventing later recovery from recreating deleted profile data. Expiry, command cancellation, and data deletion also retire the original offer controls so stale buttons cannot mislead trainers.

## Trainer profile and Kanto Gyms

`[p]pokemon profile [@member]` displays your or another server member's generated trainer card, six-slot current-party strip with sprites, names, levels, and sex marks, badge case, Pokédex progress, collection, bag, partner level/XP, and next Gym. Trainers challenge Brock through Giovanni in order. Brock is the first complete leader battle, using his Red/Blue-style Geodude Lv.12 and Onix Lv.14 team in one restart-safe challenge with per-knockout XP, automatic leader switching, and a dedicated Boulder Badge victory card. The Gym status footer shows the server’s exact `poke gym challenge` command and provides a trainer-only Challenge Leader button that starts the same validated flow. Later leaders currently retain their single-ace prototype until their full teams are implemented. Members choose their own Retro or Gold card with `[p]pokemon profilestyle retro|gold`; viewers see the trainer's saved style. Each first victory awards that leader's badge exactly once; rematches are not required for progression. Gym battles award ordinary Pokémon battle XP, cannot award or consume a caught Gym Pokémon, and occupy one server encounter slot and their channel until victory, defeat, forfeit, or expiry.

The initial Gym milestone uses each leader's signature Pokémon as a restart-safe ace challenge. Full leader teams are deliberately deferred until opponent-party support is added to the battle engine; badge storage and ordering do not need to change for that expansion.

## Pokédex styles

The Retro style is the initial bot default. The bot owner can change that with `[p]pokemonownerset pokedexstyle retro|compact`. Members can select their own style with `[p]pokemon pokedexstyle retro|compact` or return to the owner-selected default with `[p]pokemon pokedexstyle default`.

Presentation is intentionally modular: a style implements list and detail embed rendering and is registered in `POKEDEX_STYLES`. Navigation, filtering, search, generation selection, ownership checks, and unseen/seen/caught disclosure rules remain shared. Community themes must preserve those disclosure rules, remain readable on mobile, avoid proprietary assets, and retain usable embed text independent of decorative imagery. Unknown or removed style keys safely fall back to Retro.

## Boundaries

This remains a deliberately bounded playable milestone. PvP, trading, active ability/EV/gender battle mechanics, TM/HM/tutor move acquisition, stone and trade evolutions, biome/weather tables, and cross-bot ownership are deferred. The RBY level-up catalog is complete, and `move_audit.json` records the support classification and rationale for every bundled move. Deferred mechanics such as Transform, Metronome, Counter, field screens, trapping, and multi-turn charging remain explicit until each receives dedicated state and tests. Owned records already preserve ability, EV, gender, nature, origin, and catch provenance for future mechanics.
